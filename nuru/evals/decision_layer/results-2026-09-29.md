Nuru on `granite4.2:3b` through Ollama, Laya 0.3.21 on MPS, the same machine as [results-2026-09-28.md](results-2026-09-28.md). Branch `thinking-watch`; rolling it back is `git checkout laya-decision-layer`. Tests 1, 6, 7 and 9, picked from earlier runs for runaway or looping reasoning, two runs each on scratch copies of dummyVault, scored with `score_runs.py` and compared with `watch_report.py`. Only `watch_thinking` is enforced in the enforced arms (a test-only config); the real config keeps it in shadow.

## What the watch does

Every 400 thinking tokens (estimated at 4 characters each), cheapest check first: past the tier's budget (low 1,200, high 4,000), one sentence of 40+ characters seen 3 times, or Laya's `watch_thinking` answer. In shadow the first signal is logged; enforced, the stream is closed and the model asked to act with thinking off. Added during this run of evals:

- **Thinking off for one call only** after a steer; the call after that thinks as before. Keeping it off for the rest of the turn made Granite loop on tool calls.
- **The same tool with the same arguments `repeat_calls` (3) times in a turn** is a loop too: enforced, the turn stops before that call runs; in shadow it is logged once.
- **`THINK=low` is the default** (`.env.nuru`, the code default, `tiers.default`, `run_suite.py --tiers`).

## What happened

- **The watch stops the right calls, but late at high.** Granite thinks at about 20 tokens a second, so high's 4,000-token budget is about 200 s per call. The three timeouts at high were two or three such calls in a turn, each stopped only after 170-217 s. No signal in any arm came from Laya; every stop was the budget or a repeated sentence.
- **Thinking off for the rest of the turn made test 6 loop on tool calls.** In one run, 12 `search_notes` calls, then 11 `read_note` calls; in the other, the same search 10 times and `ask_an_expert` 9 times (refused after the first answer). Nothing ended either turn, as the step cap wasn't enforced. Letting thinking come back after one call fixed it: 5 and 4.
- **Shadow would have saved 96-3,214 tokens per signal** (7 repeated-sentence signals, fired at 801-3,603 tokens).
- **Low is 2.2 times faster** and as good or better on 1, 7 and 9 (49, 90 and 125 s a turn against 117, 372 and 456 s in shadow at high). **Test 6 fell to 0 and 1.** The model walked the vault note by note, stopped at the 1,200-token budget before almost every read, then re-read notes under lowercase paths (`nuru/indexes/...`), which the repeated-call stop doesn't catch, and timed out. The other run searched with a mis-escaped pattern and found 1 of 4 broken links.
- **Test 6 is a search problem more than a thinking one.** Of the two bad `find_in_notes` patterns, ` [[` returned ripgrep's own error (unclosed character class) with the escape advice; `\\[\\[.*\\]\\]` is a valid regex for a literal backslash and matched nothing, where `\[\[.*\]\]` matches 8 notes, and the model got only the generic "no line matches" hint. Even a right pattern finds links, not whether their targets exist - that takes the server's own name resolution, which `get_links` has for one note at a time.

## Found along the way

- **The session log records a failed tool call as `ok`**: the main tool loop writes `"ok"` whatever the result. The decision log has it right (`ok=0` and the error).
- **`watch_report.py` counts no steers** for the low batch, where the decision log has 8.
